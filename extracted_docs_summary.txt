Abstract: 
 
Intravenous (IV) therapy is a critical component of inpatient clinical care; however, its continuous 
monitoring remains heavily reliant on manual observation or reactive, threshold-based automated 
systems. In resource-constrained clinical environments with high patient-to-nurse ratios, this 
manual reliance exacerbates the risk of severe adverse events, such as air embolisms or undetected 
venous infiltration. Furthermore, when traditional automated systems are used, they broadcast non-
differentiated alarms only after a critical failure has occurred. This reactive approach overwhelms 
hospital floors with noise and significantly contributes to dangerous "alarm fatigue" among nursing 
staff. To solve these compounding problems, this project proposes a decentralized, predictive 
monitoring architecture. By implementing Edge AI directly on an ESP32 microcontroller paired with 
a Load Cell, the system performs real-time time-series analysis to predict flow anomalies and exact 
time-to-empty before failures occur. Additionally, a centralized AI Triage Agent ingests this 
predictive data to dynamically route prioritized, silent alerts directly to the most available staff 
member. This intelligent allocation system aims to eliminate air embolism risks and reduce 
broadcast alarm fatigue by up to 40%, safeguarding patients while optimizing nursing workflows.

Response: Across the three documents (presentation pitch, design thinking workbook, and

Here is a breakdown of the core concept:
1. The Core Problem
Air Embolism & Complications: In resource-constrained environments (like MSME
hospitals, 24/7 clinics, or floors with high patient-to-nurse ratios), IV monitoring is done
manually or using dumb/reactive systems. Empty IV bags or blocked lines go unnoticed,
leading to backflow, venous infiltration, or air embolisms.
Alarm Fatigue: Existing automated alerts are strictly reactive; they trigger loud, non-
differentiated ward buzzers after the bag is already empty or failing, causing severe noise
pollution and nursing fatigue.
High Cost of Smart Pumps: Proprietary commercial smart infusion pumps are far too
expensive ($$$) for Tier-2/Tier-3 hospitals, leaving around 85% of IV setups completely
unmonitored.
2. The Proposed Solution & Pipeline
Instead of a simple static weight threshold or a cloud-dependent system, the project proposes a
low-cost, retrofit edge-AI system that anticipates failures in advance:
1. Sense: A 1 kg Load Cell continuously measures the physical weight of the hanging IV
bag.
2. Amplify: An HX711 24-bit ADC module digitizes the load cell signal.
3. Analyze & Predict (Edge AI): An ESP32 microcontroller ingests the weight time-series
data locally and runs a TinyML model (built/trained via Edge Impulse) to classify flow
states into categories like Normal, Blocked, or Rapidly Emptying. It continuously calculates
the exact time-to-empty locally with near-zero latency.
4. Triage & Silent Alerts: The ESP32 communicates over Wi-Fi via a Blynk IoT backend /
Centralized AI Triage Agent, routing prioritized, silent push notifications to the on-duty
nurse’s mobile device rather than triggering loud room sirens.
3. Key Design Thinking & Feasibility Takeaways
Hardware Selection: Opted for a load cell + HX711 over optical drip counting (which
suffers from ambient light issues) or ultrasonic sensors (which are too expensive).
Main Risk / Assumption: Handling movement artifacts and vibrations on the IV stand so
they don't corrupt the time-series inference.
